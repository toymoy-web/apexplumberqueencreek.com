The Neighborhood

About Cortina

Cortina is in west Queen Creek, Maricopa County. Homes.com counts about 1,196 homes with a median build year of 2005. It describes Mediterranean-style houses, some ranch-style homes and townhomes, and says most homes have in-ground pools.

A Rose Law Group report on Fulton Homes calls Cortina the town's first master-planned community. Local anchors include Cortina Park, which Homes.com says has a community pool, a shaded playground, and sports courts, and Cortina Elementary School in the Higley Unified School District.

The sources we found do not publish exact boundaries for Cortina, so we do not draw a map line here. If you are not sure your street counts as Cortina, give us the nearest cross streets when you call and we will confirm that we serve it.

Home Age

What A Median Build Year Of 2005 Can Mean

A median build year of 2005 means many Cortina homes are now about twenty years old. That is not a problem in itself. It does mean that original parts are reaching the age where replacement becomes a fair question.

The U.S. Department of Energy lists 10 to 15 years as the typical life of a storage tank water heater. A home that still has its original tank is worth checking even if the water runs hot today. Faucet cartridges, toilet flappers, fill valves, and the small shutoff valves under sinks also wear with use and mineral scale.

We do not guess what is behind your walls. A technician looks at your actual fixtures, water pressure, and shutoff valves, then tells you what should be fixed now and what can wait.

Home Types

Pools, Townhomes, And Shared Lines

Homes.com says most Cortina homes have in-ground pools. Pool equipment is tied into the home's water supply, so a wet spot near the equipment pad can be a pool problem or a plumbing problem. Our pool leak detection service tests the supply lines to help sort out which one it is. We do not repair pool shells or pool equipment.

Cortina also includes townhomes, according to Homes.com. In a townhome, a shared wall or a shared line can mean your association or a neighbor is involved. Ask your HOA which lines it maintains before you schedule work. We can still help you locate where the water is coming from.

Before We Arrive

If Water Is Running At Your Cortina Home

If water is running or pooling, these steps limit the damage while you wait for a technician.

  • 1
    Close the water. Turn the stop valve under the fixture clockwise, or close the main shutoff for the house.
  • 2
    Protect the water heater. If the leak is near the heater, switch off its breaker or set a gas unit's control to off.
  • 3
    Check pool equipment. If the wet area is by the pool pump or filter, turn the pump off before you call.
  • 4
    Document the damage. Photograph wet areas for your records and move belongings away from the water.

Then call (480) 741-9332 and tell us what you see.
