Local Angle

Bigger Lots Mean More Outdoor Water

A larger lot usually comes with more outdoor water use, such as irrigation, hose bibs, and in many cases a pool. Homes.com says many Sossaman Estates homes have in-ground pools. Outdoor lines and valves sit in sun and soil year round.

A wet patch in the yard, a sprinkler zone that never seems to shut off, or a water bill that climbs without explanation deserves a leak check. A quick test is to stop all water use in the house, then see whether your water meter's leak indicator or small dial is still moving. If it is, water is going somewhere. Our leak detection service finds where.

Irrigation connections can also need a backflow device to protect your drinking water. See our backflow testing page if you are not sure whether yours is current.

Before We Arrive

If Water Is Running At Your Sossaman Estates Home

If water is running or pooling, these steps limit the damage while you wait for a technician.

  • 1
    Close the water. Turn the stop valve under the fixture clockwise, or close the main shutoff for the house.
  • 2
    Check the yard. If water is coming from the yard or an irrigation line, close the main shutoff and turn the irrigation controller off.
  • 3
    Protect the water heater. If the leak is near the heater, switch off its breaker or set a gas unit's control to off.
  • 4
    Document the damage. Photograph wet areas for your records and move belongings away from the water.
