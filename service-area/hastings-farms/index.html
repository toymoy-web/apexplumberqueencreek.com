About Hastings Farms

Homes.com lists Hastings Farms as a master-planned community in Queen Creek with about 784 homes and a median build year of 2014. It describes one- and two-story Southwestern-style homes built from the 2010s into the 2020s, on lots with a median size of about 8,276 square feet.

The community has a playground, a soccer field, and a basketball court, according to Homes.com, and the Queen Creek Wash Trail runs next to it. South Ellsworth Road runs along its western side. Listed schools are Queen Creek Elementary, Newell Barney Middle School, and Crismon High School in the Queen Creek Unified School District.

Home Age

Newer Homes Still Have Plumbing Questions

A median build year of 2014 means a typical Hastings Farms home is around a decade old. Builder warranties on plumbing generally cover a limited period, so many homes this age are past that point. Check your paperwork before you call anyone, since a covered repair is best handled through the builder.

A first failure at ten years is usually normal wear, not a sign of a defective house. Toilet fill valves, faucet cartridges, garbage disposals, and water heater parts are common wear items. A water heater is also nearing the 10 to 15 year range that the U.S. Department of Energy gives for typical tank life.

Water Quality

Scale And Your Water

Scale builds on aerators, heating elements, and tankless heat exchangers whether a home is new or old. If your home is on Town of Queen Creek water, the Town's reports have shown hardness of about 6 to 15 grains per gallon. Ask your provider which report applies to your address.

A softener or filtration system is a choice, not a requirement. We test your tap first and explain what a system would and would not do. See our pages on water softener installation, water filtration, and tankless water heater service for details.

Before We Arrive

If Water Is Running At Your Hastings Farms Home

If water is running or pooling, these steps limit the damage while you wait for a technician.

  • 1
    Close the water. Turn the stop valve under the fixture clockwise, or close the main shutoff for the house.
  • 2
    Stop an overflowing toilet. Lift the tank lid and lift the float, or close the stop valve on the wall behind the toilet.
  • 3
    Protect the water heater. If the leak is near the heater, switch off its breaker or set a gas unit's control to off.
  • 4
    Check your warranty. If the home is still under a builder warranty, note the problem and call the builder as well.

Then call (480) 741-9332 and tell us what you see.
