The Problem

Running Out Of Hot Water, Or An Error Code On The Display?

If your tankless unit isn't keeping up the way it used to, or it's showing an error code you don't recognize, that's almost always a service issue rather than a sign the unit is dying. In Queen Creek specifically, it's usually the water itself.

Annual
Recommended descaling interval in this hard-water area

Why Hard Water Is A Bigger Deal For Tankless Units

A standard tank water heater has room for scale to settle at the bottom without immediately affecting performance. A tankless unit heats water as it passes through a narrow heat exchanger, and Queen Creek's groundwater, commonly measured in the high-teens to 20-plus grains-per-gallon range, deposits scale directly on that exchanger's surface. Left long enough, that buildup reduces flow, causes inconsistent temperature, and eventually trips an error code or damages a component outright.

The fix isn't complicated. An annual descaling flush, done with a pump and a mild acid solution circulated through the unit, clears that buildup before it causes real damage, and it's a fraction of the cost of a repair or early replacement.

What A Flush Visit Looks Like

The Descaling Process

  • 1
    We connect a pump and hoses to the unit's isolation valves, which every tankless installation should have specifically for this purpose.
  • 2
    A descaling solution circulates through the heat exchanger for roughly 45 minutes to an hour, dissolving mineral buildup without needing to remove the unit.
  • 3
    We flush the solution out with clean water, then run the unit and check the flow rate and temperature response against the manufacturer's spec.
  • 4
    If the unit doesn't have isolation valves installed, we add them during the visit so future flushes are faster and less expensive.
