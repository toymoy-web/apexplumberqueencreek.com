The Problem

Water Level Dropping Faster Than It Should?

A pool that needs topping off more often than usual, a water bill that's climbed with no obvious cause, or wet spots appearing in the yard near the pool equipment. These are the calls we get most, and in Queen Creek's climate, it's easy to write the first sign off as ordinary summer evaporation until the pattern makes clear it isn't.

Why Queen Creek's Climate Makes This Common

Arizona's East Valley puts real, sustained stress on pool structures and plumbing that milder climates don't. Summer heat bakes and shifts the ground around a pool shell, and when monsoon season hits, that same soil absorbs moisture and expands rapidly. The same caliche and clay soil movement that causes slab leaks in homes across Queen Creek puts comparable stress on the pipes running between a pool and its equipment pad, and on the pool shell itself where it meets the surrounding ground.

This is a genuinely different problem from a leak inside your home's plumbing. A pool leak involves its own pressure system and its own set of connections at the equipment pad. In many cases the pool shell itself can develop hairline cracks from the same ground movement, none of which a standard household leak detection visit is set up to diagnose.

How Detection Actually Works

We start with a bucket test to confirm water loss is genuinely above normal evaporation, comparing the pool's water level against a control bucket sitting on the pool step. From there, pressure testing isolates each plumbing line running to and from the equipment pad, skimmers, and returns, identifying which specific line is losing pressure. Acoustic equipment listens for the sound of water escaping under pressure, the same underlying method used for household leak detection, applied specifically to a pool's plumbing layout. If the plumbing lines test clean, we move to inspecting the shell itself for structural cracks, often using dye testing at suspected points to visually confirm a shell leak.

None of this requires draining the pool or excavating the yard on a guess. The point of this process is narrowing the leak down to a specific line or a specific section of shell before anything gets opened up or repaired.

Community Pools And Backflow Connections

Homes in guard-gated, amenity-rich communities like Encanterra, where private pools are common alongside the community's own resort-style pools, see this issue regularly given how much pool infrastructure the area has relative to a typical Queen Creek subdivision. A pool's plumbing connection is also exactly the kind of cross-connection point Queen Creek's backflow prevention requirements exist to protect, so a pool leak visit is often a reasonable time to confirm your backflow assembly, if you have one, is current on its annual test.
