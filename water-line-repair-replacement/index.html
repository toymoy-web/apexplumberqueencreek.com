The Problem

Wet Spot In The Yard, Or A Water Bill That Doesn't Add Up?

A soggy patch of lawn between your water meter and the house, a water bill that's climbed with no indoor explanation, or water pressure that's dropped before it even reaches your home. All three point to the same place: the underground line connecting your house to the meter, not anything inside.

Meter To House
The specific line this service covers

Water Line vs. Home Plumbing: Why It Matters

Your water service line runs underground from the meter or curb stop to where it enters your house, and it's a genuinely different repair than anything happening inside the home. The same ground movement that stresses supply lines under a slab foundation, caliche and clay soil shifting as it wets and dries, puts real stress on this buried line too. It's a common, real cause of a leak that shows up as a soggy patch of yard, an unexplained rise in your water bill, or both at once.

We locate the leak with acoustic equipment before recommending a fix, the same approach we use for any hidden leak, so you're not paying to dig up more yard than necessary.

What To Expect During A Water Line Repair

Once we've pinpointed the leak, we mark the access area and confirm whether trenchless repair is viable for that specific spot and pipe material. If it is, we access through small pits at either end of the damaged section rather than trenching the full run. Water service is typically restored the same day for a standard repair, with backfilling and any landscaping restoration following once the repair is confirmed sound.

Low Pressure Isn't Always A Leak

A drop in water pressure can mean a leak, but it can also mean a partially closed valve at the meter, mineral buildup narrowing the line itself over years, or an issue on the utility side that isn't yours to fix. We check pressure at the meter and at the house to narrow down where the problem actually sits before assuming the worst and recommending a repair you may not need. That check alone resolves a real share of the low-pressure calls we get, without ever needing to dig anywhere.

Frequently Asked Questions

The water line, sometimes called the service line, runs underground from the water meter or curb stop to your house. Everything past where it enters the home is internal plumbing, covered separately under repiping. A leak in the service line shows up as a wet spot in the yard between the meter and the house, or a water bill that's up with no indoor cause.

Where the line's condition allows it, we use trenchless methods similar to sewer line work, accessing the line through small pits rather than an open trench along its full length.

Generally, the line from the meter to your house is the homeowner's responsibility; the utility maintains the line up to and including the meter. We can help confirm exactly where a specific leak falls.

The same caliche and clay soil movement that affects slab foundations puts stress on buried water lines too, along with ordinary age and, occasionally, a line that was damaged during nearby digging or landscaping work.

A trenchless replacement typically takes one day for a standard residential line. Traditional excavation can take one to two days depending on length and depth.
