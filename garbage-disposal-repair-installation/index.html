What Not To Put Down A Disposal

Even a well-maintained disposal isn't built for everything. Fibrous foods like celery, corn husks, and onion skins are the most common jam cause we see, since the stringy fibers wrap around the flywheel instead of getting cut.

Coffee grounds and eggshells don't jam the unit, but they can build up in the drain line below it over time, which shows up later as a slow-draining sink rather than a disposal problem. Grease and cooking oil should never go down any drain, disposal or not, since it solidifies and coats the inside of your pipes as it cools.

None of this means you need to babysit the unit constantly. Running cold water for a few seconds before and after use, and avoiding the items above, is genuinely enough to keep most disposals working well for years.

How Long A Disposal Actually Lasts

Most residential disposals are built to last 10 to 15 years with normal use, though the motor and seals are what typically fail first, not the grinding components themselves.

If your unit is in that age range and starting to act up, replacement is usually the more sensible call than a repair, since a motor or seal repair on an older unit tends to be a short-term fix at best. For a unit under 5 years old, a repair almost always makes more sense.
