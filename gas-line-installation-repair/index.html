Smell Gas? Here's What To Do

A rotten-egg smell is the mercaptan additive utilities put into natural gas specifically so leaks are noticeable, since natural gas itself is odorless. If you smell it, leave the house immediately without operating any switches or electronics, don't try to locate the leak yourself, and call from outside. This is a genuine emergency, not something to wait on.

Sizing Matters

Why BTU Load Determines The Line

Every gas appliance has a BTU demand rating, and the gas line feeding it has to be sized to deliver that much fuel at the right pressure, especially if it's sharing a branch line with other appliances. An undersized line can starve a high-demand appliance like a tankless water heater or a professional-style range, causing it to underperform or fail to ignite properly even though the appliance itself is fine. We calculate total demand on the line, not just the new appliance, before sizing anything.

Southwest Gas And Your Property

Southwest Gas owns and maintains the meter and the main supply line up to your property. Everything past the meter, the piping that actually distributes gas to your appliances, is the homeowner's responsibility and our scope of work. If a leak or issue turns out to be on the utility side of the meter, we'll tell you that directly and point you to Southwest Gas rather than attempting work that isn't ours to do. That line is outside any plumber's scope regardless of who you call, and we're happy to help you figure out which side a given issue falls on.

For new construction or a significant addition, coordinating the timing between our line work and the utility's meter set or upgrade is part of the job, and we handle that coordination rather than leaving you to manage two separate parties.

Outdoor Living And Gas Lines

Queen Creek's outdoor living culture means a real share of our gas line calls are for features added after the original build: an outdoor kitchen, a fire pit, a patio heater, or a pool heater. Running a new branch line for any of these means tapping into the existing system at the right point and sizing correctly for the added demand, which is worth planning with a plumber before the hardscape or patio work is finished, not after.
