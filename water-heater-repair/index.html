Water Heater Emergency: What To Do Before We Arrive

A leaking water heater can empty a tank onto a garage or closet floor within minutes, so the first goal is to stop the water and remove the power source. Most calls on this topic start with a puddle someone noticed late, or a heater that went cold overnight.

  • 1
    Shut off the cold water supply valve on top of the heater. That stops the tank from refilling while it leaks.
  • 2
    Turn off the power. Switch the breaker off for an electric unit, or turn the gas control to off for a gas unit.
  • 3
    Stay clear of the relief valve pipe. Water from that discharge pipe can be scalding.
  • 4
    Move boxes and stored items away from the water, then call our 24/7 line so we can arrive while the heater is still isolated.

A leak from a fitting is often a same-visit repair. Water seeping from the body of the tank usually means the tank itself has failed, and the U.S. Department of Energy lists 10 to 15 years as the typical life of a storage tank water heater. Common Symptoms

What's Actually Wrong

  • 1
    No hot water at all usually means a failed heating element, a tripped thermostat, or, on gas units, a pilot light or igniter problem.
  • 2
    Hot water that runs out fast often points to a bad lower heating element or sediment that's shrunk the tank's usable capacity.
  • 3
    Popping or rumbling sounds are almost always mineral scale built up on the bottom of the tank, a very common issue given how hard Queen Creek's water runs.
  • 4
    Rust-colored water or a leak at the base usually means the tank itself is corroding internally, which is a replacement sign more than a repair.

Gas vs. Electric: Different Diagnostic Path

A gas water heater with no hot water usually traces back to a pilot light that's gone out, a faulty thermocouple, or an igniter that isn't sparking on demand-ignition models. We check the gas valve and venting as part of that diagnosis, since a venting problem can shut a unit down as a safety measure.

An electric unit instead comes down to the upper or lower heating element, the thermostat, or, less often, a tripped high-limit switch that needs manually resetting once the underlying cause is fixed.

Knowing which type you have before we arrive helps, but it isn't required. Our technicians carry parts for both gas and electric units on the truck for the most common failure points, so most repairs finish in a single visit regardless of which kind you have. On every gas unit, we test the temperature-and-pressure relief valve as standard practice before considering the repair complete, the same safety check called for under Queen Creek's adopted plumbing code.
