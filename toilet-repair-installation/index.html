Overflowing Or Leaking

Toilet Emergency: Stop The Water First

Most toilet emergencies can be slowed in seconds if you know where to reach. The goal is to stop the water first and sort out the cause second.

  • 1
    Close the supply valve on the wall behind the toilet by turning it clockwise. That stops new water from entering the tank.
  • 2
    Do not flush again. Lift the tank lid and push the flapper down to stop flow into a rising bowl.
  • 3
    Put towels around the base and protect finished flooring and any ceiling below.
  • 4
    If other fixtures gurgle or back up, stop using water and call, because the problem is in the drain line and not the toilet.

For a toilet that only runs, the Town of Queen Creek suggests a dye test, with a few drops of food coloring in the tank and a check of the bowl after 15 to 20 minutes. EPA WaterSense uses 10 minutes. If color shows up in the bowl, the flapper is leaking. Call (480) 741-9332.

Common Issues

What Usually Needs Fixing

  • 1
    A toilet that runs constantly or intermittently almost always means the fill valve or flapper isn't sealing, which quietly wastes water and shows up on your bill.
  • 2
    A weak or incomplete flush can come from a partially clogged trap, a worn flapper not lifting fully, or, on an older unit, mineral buildup restricting the rim jets.
  • 3
    A toilet that rocks or feels loose at the base usually means the wax ring has failed, which can let water leak down into the subfloor if it isn't caught.
  • 4
    Cracks in the tank or bowl are not repairable and mean it's time for a full replacement rather than a patch.

Choosing Between Standard And Comfort-Height

A standard toilet bowl sits around 15 inches from floor to rim, while a comfort-height, sometimes called "chair height," model runs closer to 17 to 19 inches, matching the height of a typical dining chair.

Comfort-height units are the more common choice now in new installations generally, since most people find them easier to sit down on and stand up from, and they're often the default option at most price points today. Taller household members in particular tend to prefer them.

For accessibility needs specifically, we can install ADA-compliant toilets, which meet a defined height range along with grab-bar clearance requirements, and we can talk through what that means for your specific bathroom layout before the work starts.

Real Cost Factors

A few things move the price on any toilet job: whether the shut-off valve under the toilet also needs replacing (common on an older fixture), whether the flooring or subfloor shows any sign of past water damage, and which model you choose. Comfort-height and higher-efficiency units simply cost more than a basic standard bowl.
