Water Under The Floor

Slab Leak Emergency: Steps To Take Right Now

Callers describe slab leaks as warm floors, the sound of running water in a quiet house, or water coming up under baseboards and floorboards. A pressurized line under a slab does not stop on its own, so the goal is to cut the supply and protect the home.

  • 1
    Shut off the main water valve and watch the meter. It should stop if the leak is on the pressurized side.
  • 2
    Turn off the water heater if the floor feels warm, because a hot spot often points to a hot supply line.
  • 3
    Lift rugs and move furniture off wet flooring, and run a fan if you have one.
  • 4
    Photograph the area and call us. Insurance carriers generally want documentation of the damage before cleanup.

Tell the dispatcher what you see and hear. If the leak is sending water into the house, we treat it as an emergency visit. Real pricing comes after we locate the break, and we explain every repair option before any concrete is cut. Call (480) 741-9332.

Why This Happens Even In Newer Homes

It's a question we get on almost every slab leak call: how can a house built within the last decade already have this problem? The honest answer is that slab leaks here are mostly a soil issue, not a construction-quality issue.

Queen Creek's caliche layer is dense and doesn't compress evenly, and as it absorbs and releases moisture across the seasons, especially during monsoon season, it shifts in ways that put uneven pressure on rigid supply lines running beneath the foundation.

A copper line under that kind of stress can develop a pinhole leak at a joint or a bend point well before it would fail from ordinary wear. PEX, which is more common in newer construction, handles some flexing better but isn't immune either, particularly at its fittings. Neither material failing early is a defect, it's simply what the ground here does over time.
