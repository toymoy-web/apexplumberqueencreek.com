Water Under The Sink

Faucet And Sink Emergency: Shut The Water Off First

Failed supply lines and stuck shutoff valves are common reasons homeowners call us in a hurry. The fix is quick for a plumber, but the first minute is about stopping the water.

  • 1
    Turn the small stop valves under the sink clockwise to cut water to the faucet. If they will not turn, do not force them.
  • 2
    If the stops fail, close the main shutoff valve for the house.
  • 3
    Empty the cabinet, set a bucket under the leak, and mop up standing water to protect the cabinet floor.
  • 4
    Call us. A valve that will not close is a repair we can usually handle in one visit.

Hard water is tough on valves, since scale can seize a stop valve that has sat untouched for years. That is why a shutoff that will not close is a common reason for a late-night call. Call (480) 741-9332.

Why Faucets Wear Out Faster In Queen Creek

Queen Creek's groundwater runs very hard, and every time water passes through a faucet, it leaves behind a small amount of mineral scale on the internal cartridge and inside the aerator at the tip. Over time that buildup is what causes a drip a simple washer swap won't fix, or a noticeable drop in flow that has nothing to do with your home's water pressure.

It's a normal part of owning a home here, not a sign anything was installed wrong. Most faucets in hard-water areas like this one need a cartridge replaced every few years, well before the fixture itself would otherwise need replacing.

Sink Installation

Drop-In, Undermount, Or Farmhouse

A drop-in sink sits into a cut opening with its rim visible on the counter, the simplest and least expensive option to install or swap.

An undermount sink mounts beneath the counter from below for a cleaner look and easier counter cleanup, but it requires a counter material that can support the mounting hardware, like stone or solid surface. A farmhouse or apron-front sink extends past the cabinet face and typically needs cabinet modifications if you're converting from a standard sink.

Kitchen Vs. Bathroom Repairs

A kitchen faucet works harder than a bathroom one: more volume, hotter water on average, and more exposure to food debris and grease near the sprayer and base seal. Kitchen faucets in Queen Creek tend to need a cartridge or sprayer hose replaced sooner as a result. Bathroom faucets fail more often from simple mineral buildup on the aerator or a slow drip from a worn cartridge, since they typically see lighter daily use.

Either way, we carry stock cartridges and aerators for the most common brands sold in this market, which is usually what keeps a repair to one visit instead of two.

If a specific part isn't something we carry on the truck, we'll tell you that upfront along with a realistic timeline to source it, rather than leaving you guessing about when the repair will actually be finished.

Shower Valves And Tub Faucets That Will Not Shut Off

The same crews that fix kitchen faucets get calls about shower and tub fixtures, and the symptoms are usually plain. A shower that drips for hours after you turn it off, a tub spout that runs hot and cold at once, or a damp patch behind a bathroom wall below the valve all point to a failing valve cartridge or a leaking connection.

A cartridge swap is a short repair when the trim is accessible. If the leak is inside the wall, we open a small access panel to reach the valve body, and we tell you before we cut anything. A shower valve that leaks into the wall is worth fixing promptly, since it can soak drywall for days before a stain shows.
